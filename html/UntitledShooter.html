Untitled Shooter Project

About the project

Untitled Shooter Project is a single-player, retro-inspired first-person shooter that I am developing in Unity as a personal project. It is my attempt to better execute the idea behind Dual Wield, my university group project: a shooter where you hold a weapon in each hand, and the pairing you choose matters more than either gun on its own. The game is in early development, so everything shown here is a work-in-progress prototype rather than a finished product.

Revisiting the idea

Unlike my other projects, this one isn't a university assignment, which gives me the freedom to return to Dual Wield and fix what our playtesters told us didn't work. In Dual Wield both guns fired on one button and drew from a shared ammo pool, which was the most criticised system in the game. Now each hand fires on its own button while sharing a single aim point, and every weapon tracks its own ammo. There is no reloading: when a weapon runs dry, the answer is to swap it for another and change the pairing, and because enemies drop ammo when they die, you resupply by fighting rather than backtracking.

Designing the pairings

Every pair of weapons, including two of the same, has its own hand-designed combo that changes how the two weapons behave together rather than just adding damage. A leaf blower can curve your own rocket onto your crosshair, an energy beam can detonate that rocket in mid-air, and an enemy frozen by the cryo caster shatters into a storm of shards when you hit it with the beam. The enemies are designed to test your pairing too: fast melee enemies ask whether you have a way to knock them back, and armoured heavies shrug off shotgun pellets but crack under explosives. Movement is fast but grounded, so skill comes from what you shoot with rather than how fast you move.

Progress so far

So far I have built a playable prototype mission with six combat arenas, six weapons, 21 weapon combinations and four enemy types. Each new weapon is handed to you in both hands, so your first impression of it is a pairing rather than a single gun, and the name of your current combo stays on the HUD so you always know what your two hands will do.
